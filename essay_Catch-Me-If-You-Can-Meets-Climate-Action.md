The SFOBLS—A Lock. A Legacy. A Little Audacity. 
(A confident, charismatic figure steps onto a virtual Davos stage, the Golden Gate Bridge 
glowing behind them—but wait… is that a second, sleeker structure beside it? The crowd leans 
in.) 
Good morning, global elites, clipboard-clutching bureaucrats, and at least three people who 
definitely skimmed my proposal in the Uber over. We’re here to talk about the 
future—specifically, how to avoid one where San Francisco’s waterfront becomes Atlantis with 
better sourdough. 
Let’s cut to the chase: 
The San Francisco Bay Oceanic Barrier & Lock System (SFOBLS) isn’t just infrastructure. It’s a 
heist movie plot against Mother Nature. 

- Ocean rising? Lock it out.
- Storm surge? Pump it into renewable energy.
- Bored commuters? Add a hyperloop.
This is the Golden Gate Bridge 2.0—because in Silicon Valley, we don’t just disrupt apps. We 
disrupt apocalypses. 
Why This? Why Now? 
(Or: “The Oceans Are Coming—Act Like It.”) 
Today’s tsunami warning? A preview. 
NOAA’s latest projections show a 2-foot sea rise swallowing SFO’s runways, Facebook’s 
espresso machines, and your aunt’s Alameda condo. The “do nothing” plan leads to $3 trillion in 
losses, mass tech migration to Denver, and crabs in little life jackets. 
But here’s the twist: 
SFOBLS turns defense into offense. 
- Economic Supercharger: Protects $1.3 trillion in GDP and monetizes tidal energy. Green 
bonds? More like golden bonds.
- Global Blueprint: If we nail it, Shanghai and New York will copy us faster than a startup 
clones Uber.
- A Legacy That’s Not Just Floodwater: Picture the year 2225: “They built a bridge, then 
they built a lock—and saved the coast.”

Tsunamis? We’ve Got Receipts 
San Francisco’s had three big tsunami scares since 1906 (thanks, Alaska and Japan). Each 
time, the Bay’s tight entrance saved us—mostly. But with sea rise, the next one could turn 
Mission Bay into Mission Baywatch. 
SFOBLS is a tsunami speedbump. 
The Closing Argument (With Jazz Hands) 
This isn’t just about concrete. It’s about daring to out-engineer the ocean. 
The Reber Plan failed in the 1940s. They lacked our tech, our urgency, and—let’s be 
honest—our flair. 
So, world leaders: 
Let’s build a lock so bold, even DiCaprio’s agent called first. Or we can keep debating until the 
only thing left to save is a floating TikTok of the Bay Bridge.
