## The baby step

Most of the time the wall is a bathtub with low walls. The levers stand open, parallel to the flow. At slack water, when the levels on both sides are about equal, they twist closed for the cost of turning, and for a few hours of storm surge or king tide the Bay stops rising. Design ceiling about 20 to 22 feet of head; above that it calms things rather than stops them. Someday it can be locked in and welded. For now, levers.

What one wall buys, in the author's list: hydro energy, hyperbaric wound care, cold compute, a Faraday pod, and tourism. Rent a lever. It turns, and you turn with it.

## Status

Idea and essay. Not engineering. The model's numbers are illustrative hydrostatics, not a hydrodynamic study of the Gate. Nothing here is a claim of buildability.
